## Cycle semantics, in prose

**Insert.** `ins_ready` is a function of the registered occupancy alone: 0
exactly when the queue is full. It does **not** consult `grant_ready`, so no
combinational path runs from the functional unit back to dispatch, and a full
queue costs one cycle of dispatch even in the cycle it drains. The slot taken is
the first free one at or after the allocation pointer, wrapping by explicit
comparison.

**Wakeup.** One per-cycle broadcast. A hit on an entry requires the tag *and*
the generation to match, and requires the operand to be not-ready already, so a
second broadcast for a satisfied operand cannot overwrite it. The broadcast also
reaches an entry being *offered* on the insert port in the same cycle, and the
value it substitutes is the broadcast's, not the insert bus's.

**Select.** Among resident entries that are ready *after* this cycle's broadcast
and have not already been presented, the oldest by age wins. If none is, the
entry being offered this cycle is considered — it is always the youngest, so it
loses to anything resident. Age comparison is modular, and its correctness is
the subject of the next section.

**Grant hold.** A presented-but-unaccepted entry is excluded from selection, so
it can be neither lost nor issued twice, and its payload cannot move: a
broadcast cannot touch a ready operand, and no other event writes a ready
entry's fields. The entry leaves the queue on the accepting edge and not before.

**Kill.** Removes every live uop of the named macro and, with `kill_younger,
everything younger than the oldest of them. Kill has priority over an
outstanding grant: a kill naming the presented entry withdraws the grant in that
cycle, because a squashed uop must not issue. A kill naming a macro that is not
resident removes nothing.

**Reset.** Synchronous, active high. Clears the valid vector, the granted
vector, the occupancy, the allocation pointer, the age counter and the counters.
It clears nothing else: the entry arrays are register state, not RAM, and
clearing `DEPTH` of them would be a full-array reset, the rule
`rtl/common/mosaic_ram.sv` documents against. `ins_ready` and `grant_valid` are
forced low while `rst` is high, so no transfer is *reported* during a reset
cycle.

## The age, and the inequality

The age is a wrapping counter. The plan requires its modulus to exceed twice
the largest distance two live entries can be separated by, and says a modulus
*equal* to that bound is a defect. From the header of `mosaic_iq.sv`:

```
largest distance between two live entries = DEPTH - 1 = 7
required:  MOD > 2 * (DEPTH - 1)  =  14
chosen:    MOD = 2**AGE_W = 2**$clog2(4*DEPTH) = 2**5 = 32  >  14
```

That bound is **structural**, not an assumption. The live ages are always a
contiguous window `{B, …, B+n-1}` with `n <= DEPTH`, maintained by two rules:

1. a removal closes its hole — every survivor steps down one for each removed
   entry older than it, so the window never grows;
2. when the removed entry *was* the base, the survivors are **not** stepped down
   for it, so the window base slides up instead.

Rule 2 is what makes the wrap reachable at all. Without it the age field never
wraps, the modular comparison is never exercised, and a width that is too narrow
passes every test that does not happen to build a full window. With it, a queue
that issues one entry per cycle walks its base round the modulus in 32 cycles.
`AGE_MOD_TOO_SMALL` is an elaboration-time guard: a width at or below
`2*(DEPTH-1)` references a module that does not exist and fails the build rather
than passing a comment.

The generation width is derived the same way: a broadcast is raised only after
the value is durably written and a tag cannot be recycled until its previous
owner retires, so a broadcast can be in flight for at most as many recycles of
one tag as there are instructions in flight — bounded by `MOSAIC_ROB_ENTRIES`.
Hence `2**TAG_GEN_W > MOSAIC_ROB_ENTRIES`, i.e. 128 > 64.

## State kept, and why

`slot_valid` and `slot_granted` are explicit packed vectors outside the entry
arrays: validity masks the payload, so the payload does not need a reset, and
`granted` is what makes the back-pressure hold a property of the arbitration
rather than only of the output wires. The entry arrays (uop id, operation,
immediate, two sources with their tags, generations, readiness and values, one
destination, and the age) are register state: a slot is written by an insertion,
written again by any number of broadcasts, and read by the grant, the kill
matcher and the observation port in arbitrary order. That is multi-port register
state, so the reads are combinational reads of registers, which is what the
silicon does. `ent_age` is in the "not reset" list on purpose: it is meaningless
without its valid bit, and resetting it would duplicate information the valid
vector already carries.

## Invariants exported, and asserted

```
o_ins_total == o_grant_total + o_kill_total + o_count
```

checked by the shadow on every cycle, against the shadow's own tallies. Plus,
per cycle: the valid vector, the occupancy, `o_full`, `o_age_ctr` against the
shadow's unbounded counter reduced modulo the age width, the four broadcast
counters summing to `o_wu_total`, `o_dst_conflict` against an independent
duplicate-destination search, the grant payload field by field, and the
observation port swept over all eight slots with every field compared —
including ages, readiness, and now the stored source tags and generations.

The age **window** invariant is checked, not assumed: every cycle the shadow
asserts that the live ages span exactly `count - 1`. That is the assertion that
makes the design's safety claim testable, and it is what catches a window that
has been allowed to grow.

## Testbench: independence of the shadow

The shadow keeps a 64-bit **absolute** age per entry that never wraps, and orders
by plain `<`. The DUT's age is five bits and its comparison is modular. The two
share no expression, so a DUT whose age logic is inverted cannot agree with the
checker — and it did not: writing the modular comparison the other way round
(`!gap[MSB]` instead of `gap[MSB]`) is a one-character RTL defect that this
structure catches immediately, because an entry one step *younger* than its
neighbour then reads as the older one. That defect was found and fixed during
this work; the mutation table below would have caught it too.

Two `mosaic_iq` instances are driven, one per cluster, each against its own
shadow, with different seeds in the randomised phase. That is the card's "local
FUs only" as an observation rather than an assertion.

## Where it stands

The RTL and the shadow agree, cycle by cycle, through the geometry phase, the
reset phase, insert-basic, **oldest-ready (not lowest index)**, **same-cycle
enqueue and wakeup**, and **producer-older-than-consumer**. Those three directed
phases are the ones the card names, and they pass.

### What was fixed since the first report

Three real defects, two in the testbench and one in the RTL:

1. **RTL — `IsOlder` was inverted** (already noted). Fixed; the header now
   derives the comparison rather than asserting it.
2. **Driver — stimulus wider than the ports.** The driver was building tags and
   destination tags wider than the 7-bit ports, so the DUT saw the truncated
   value and the shadow the untruncated one. This surfaced as a bogus
   `o_dst_conflict` disagreement and, because the divergence was on a *stored*
   field, cascaded through every later phase. Fixed by clamping every stimulus
   field to its port width once, in `Bench::Step`, so the shadow and the
   hardware are provably looking at the same number. That is also the reason the
   `o_dst_conflict` check was the right place to look first.
3. **Driver — phases that queue entries without holding the FU.** A fill with
   `grant_ready` left at its default `true` issues each uop the cycle it is
   dispatched, so the queue never fills and the phase asserts about a state it
   never built. Fixed with an explicit `Bench::Hold()` rather than by changing
   the default, so a phase that wants the accept path still has to say so.

Also fixed: a real drain (`Bench::DrainQueue`) — a run of idle cycles cannot
empty a queue, because a *blocked* entry is never granted — and the grant
stability check no longer fires on a cycle that issues a kill, since a kill
naming the presented entry is the one documented exception to "the grant holds
until accepted" and the shadow already validates the withdrawal itself.

### The edge-alignment defect, and the two snapshots

The phase-level assertions were reading the **live** top-level ports after
`Step` had already run the clock edge, so they compared the hardware's *next*
cycle against the shadow's *current* one. A grep settles this class of question
in one command:

```
$ grep -nE 'rep\.Check\(!?bench\.top\(\)->c[01]_' sim/unit/tb_iq.cpp
  -> 9 sites, 3 of them failing outright and 6 passing by luck (state that does
     not change across the edge: o_full, ins_ready)
$ grep -c 'bench.top()->c' sim/unit/tb_iq.cpp     # 0 after the conversion
```

There are two correct snapshots and they answer different questions, so both
exist and neither is used for the other's question:

- `seen_*` — read **during** the per-cycle check, i.e. *before* the edge. This is
  "what the DUT presented on the cycle under test". A grant payload belongs
  here.
- `post_*` — read **after** `Edge()`, once the hardware has settled and the
  shadow has been advanced across the same edge. This is "what is true now":
  occupancies, counters, `o_dst_conflict`, `o_full`, `ins_ready`.

Mixing them is the bug, and it is the same mistake the `seen_*` accessors were
introduced to fix; it had simply not been applied to every call site.
